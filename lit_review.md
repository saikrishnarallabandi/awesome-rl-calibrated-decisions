# Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions: A Literature Review (DRAFT v0.3, 2026-10-21)

> Draft. Not yet published. Covers 79 works (2017–2026 + one 2007 canonical reference), each with a resolving URL; venue status per row in papers.csv.

## 1. Scope and anchor: TypeSafe AI's Jev
**Verified facts (primary source):** TypeSafe AI ("Introducing System One Models & Jev", Diogo Almeida, founder, Sep 15 2026, https://typesafe.ai/blog/introducing-system-one-models-and-jev) introduces *System One Models*: models that do not generate text but return **typed probabilistic decisions** (choice / score / probability) for software to act on. Training method is called **Reinforcement Learning for Calibrated Decisions (RLCD)**, contrasted with RLHF/RLVR, optimizing for "answers with epistemically honest probabilities". Claims: ~two orders of magnitude faster/cheaper than LLMs on System-One tasks, "can't hallucinate" (no string output).
**Secondary coverage:** InfoQ (Oct 1 2026) — 32K context, $0.042/M input tokens, 70–500 ms latency, Vercel AI Gateway/LangChain integrations; TechCrunch (Sep 18 2026); Arize (Jev vs LLM judges); TrueStandard and tsukumo.ch independent tests (tsukumo calls the 193×/445× marketing numbers into question); OpenChamber tweet analysis reports median user-reported speedup ~7× vs 193.6× headline (via InfoQ).
**Independent research:** Deußer, Sparrenberg, Sifa, *Evaluating and Benchmarking the System One Model Jev*, arXiv 2609.37647 (zero-shot on 37 datasets); V. Dibia, *How Jev works: calibrated decision models* (explainer, https://victordibia.com/papers/jev.pdf); M. Luo, *Jev and Typed Decision Models: An Empirical Survey* (draft, not peer-reviewed, https://eurekaleo.github.io/awesome-jev-survey/paper/main.pdf). A *Calibration-Aware RL for LLMs* survey site exists (https://yubol-bobo.github.io/rlcd-survey/).
**Not verified:** RLCD algorithm details (TypeSafe has published no technical paper we found); "can't hallucinate" and speed multipliers are vendor claims. Note: an "awesome-jev-survey" already exists — positioning must differ (see README options).

## 4. Synthesis by part

### Part 1. Foundations: calibration & verbal confidence
Neural nets and LMs are miscalibrated in predictable ways but measurable and fixable post hoc (Guo; Kadavath). Verbal confidence can be learned (Lin; Mielke) and semantic entropy gives free-text uncertainty (Kuhn). Selective classification (Geifman) is the decision-theoretic root of abstention. These works define the metrics (ECE, Brier, AUROC, risk-coverage) every later paper reuses.

- [On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599) (2017; ICML 2017 (PMLR v70))
- [Selective Classification for Deep Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08500) (2017; NeurIPS 2017)
- [Reducing conversational agents’ overconfidencethrough linguistic calibration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.14983) (2020; TACL 2022 (from memory; not re-verified today))
- [Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14334) (2022; TMLR 2022 (OpenReview forum))
- [Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221) (2022; arXiv preprint (Anthropic); no venue version found)
- [Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664) (2023; ICLR 2023 (notable top 25%))

### Part 2. How alignment/RLHF affects calibration
Consistent finding: post-training sharpens and inflates confidence (GPT-4 report; He et al.; Kadavath shows a temperature fix suffices for RLHF policies). Tian et al. (full read) show verbalized confidence from RLHF-LMs is ~50% relatively better calibrated than token probabilities. Oh & Hwang caution the effect is metric/extraction-dependent; sycophancy-inducing GRPO causes calibration collapse (2604.10585).

- [GPT-4 Technical Report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08774) (2023; arXiv technical report (OpenAI))

### Part 3. RL with calibration-aware (scoring-rule) rewards
The technical core of 'RL for calibrated decisions'. Reward = correctness + strictly proper scoring rule on stated confidence: log score (Rewarding Doubt, ICLR'26), Brier (RLCR, ICLR'26, with a proof that bounded proper rules yield accurate+calibrated optima), decision-based log loss on reader forecasts (Band et al., ICML'24). Taming Overconfidence (ICLR'25) fixes the reward model rather than the reward. 2607.04332 shows naive correct/incorrect-split rewards are hackable; 2607.00164 finds proper rewards still degrade calibration for aleatoric forecasting — design details matter.

### Part 4. Calibration in RLVR / reasoning models
Binary-correctness RLVR/GRPO systematically degrades calibration. 2026 fixes: uncertainty-aware advantage (CAPO, ACL'26), decoupled objectives resolving a gradient conflict (ICML'26), process-level confidence margins (RLCM), semantic rewards, differentiable readouts that avoid verbal-confidence collapse, and diagnosis of concentrated confidence priors that stall calibration gradients. Most evidence is math/QA, in-domain.

### Part 5. Abstention & hallucination-aware RL
Ternary rewards (+1 / 0 / −λ) are the dominant recipe: TruthRL (ICML'26; up to −28.9% hallucination), Reinforced Hesitation (Pareto frontier over λ), Intellectual Humility, Abstain-R1 (Findings ACL'26), KARL. Binary retrieval rewards (ICML'26) and pay-per-search penalties are implicit abstention signals. Evaluation work (MedAbstain EACL'26, RiskEval) shows models' stated confidence is not cost-aware. Open variable: λ should come from downstream cost.

### Part 8. Theory / position
Gneiting & Raftery (2007) supply the proper-scoring-rule theory that justifies Brier/log rewards. Kalai et al. (2025) argue binary-graded benchmarks reward guessing and hence cause hallucination. BAS proposes an answer-or-abstain utility metric. Together they argue for evaluating and training on decision utility rather than accuracy alone.

- [Why Language Models Hallucinate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04664) (2025; arXiv preprint (OpenAI); no venue version found)
- [BAS: A Decision-Theoretic Approach to Evaluating Large Language Model Confidence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03216) (2026; arXiv preprint; no venue found in OpenReview/ACL/PMLR/proceedings search)
- [Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) (2007; Journal of the American Statistical Association 102(477):359–378)

### Part 10. Jev / System One models
TypeSafe's Jev operationalizes RLCD as a non-generative typed-decision API. Independent evidence (Deußer et al., full read): strong zero-shot accuracy and well-calibrated choice probabilities supporting selective prediction, but binary probabilities poorly placed at 0.5. A fast-growing preprint cluster (Sep–Oct 2026) probes adversarial robustness (JevAdvBench), coherence with probability axioms, framing sensitivity/cost (TypedBench), risk-budgeted fallbacks, and narrow domain variants. None are peer-reviewed yet; RLCD itself has no public technical paper.

## 5. Open problems
1. No public technical description of RLCD; no open reproducible 'decision-only' calibrated baseline.
2. Calibration under distribution shift after RL (most results in-domain math/QA).
3. Verbal vs latent confidence: sampled verbal confidence collapses to few values; readouts need weights access.
4. Abstention penalty λ chosen ad hoc rather than from downstream cost (Reinforced Hesitation's own limitation).
5. Agentic calibration: per-step vs episode-level confidence.
6. Metrics: ECE is binning-sensitive; report proper scores + selective risk-coverage + coherence (2609.33209) together.
7. Confidence reward hacking (2607.04332) and sycophancy-driven calibration collapse.
8. Calibration ≠ coherence ≠ robustness for typed decision models (JevAdvBench, TypedBench).
